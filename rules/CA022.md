# CA022: a rotor is assigned to a tilt servo that does not exist

Level: warning

## What it checks

`CA_ROTORn_TILT` assigns a rotor to a tilt servo: 0 means the rotor does not
tilt, 1 is the first tilt servo (`CA_SV_TL0_*`), 2 the second, and so on. The
rule reports a rotor whose `CA_ROTORn_TILT` is larger than `CA_SV_TL_COUNT`,
the number of tilt servos.

It applies to the airframes for which PX4 reads `CA_ROTORn_TILT`: tiltrotor
VTOL (`CA_AIRFRAME` 3) and multirotor with tilt (`CA_AIRFRAME` 8). Only rotors
below `CA_ROTOR_COUNT` are checked; the others are covered by
[CA021](CA021.md).

## Why it matters

PX4 handles such a rotor like one with `CA_ROTORn_TILT` 0, without a message
(`ActuatorEffectivenessRotors::updateAxisFromTilts()` and
`ActuatorEffectivenessTilts::updateTorqueSign()` in PX4 v1.17.0):

- The rotor does not count for the yaw torque of any tilt servo.
- On a tiltrotor its thrust axis does not follow the tilt, and it is on the
  list of rotors that PX4 stops in forward flight
  (`ActuatorEffectivenessTiltrotorVTOL::setFlightPhase()`).

The matrices in the report are computed with the tilt servos in the hover
position. With the default thrust axis a tilting and a non-tilting rotor have
the same column there, so the matrix of the motors can look reasonable although
the assignment is wrong.

The statements above come from reading the PX4 code and from the matrices of
the example. The behaviour in flight was not tested.

## Example

`examples/tiltrotor_tilt_servo_missing.params` is `examples/tiltrotor.params`
with `CA_SV_TL_COUNT` 1 in place of 2. Rotor 0 is assigned to tilt servo 2,
which no longer exists. The finding names motor0. Compared with
`examples/tiltrotor.params` the report has one tilt servo less, and half the
yaw authority in matrix 0; the columns of the four motors are the same.

## What to do

Set `CA_SV_TL_COUNT` to the number of tilt servos, or correct
`CA_ROTORn_TILT`. If the rotor is meant not to tilt, set `CA_ROTORn_TILT` to 0.
